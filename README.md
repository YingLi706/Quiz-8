## Part 1: Imaging Technique Inspiration

### Chosen Example: Ding Yi — Appearance of Crosses, with reference to Anni Albers’ woven textiles

<img width="3508" height="2480" alt="ding-yi-print" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4acea646-cd07-4cbd-be59-e71172a50d3f" />

<img width="3508" height="2480" alt="anni-albers-weaving" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/71d5134c-740c-4702-a357-cf0efce04273" />

I am inspired by Ding Yi’s Appearance of Crosses series, especially his repeated use of “+” and “x” marks across a grid. I also connect this to Anni Alber‘s woven textile patterns, where repeated threads create rhythm and surface texture. What I want to borrow is the stitched, interlaced visual quality: simple marks become layered, textile like images. This technique is useful for my project because code could shift the density, colour and position of the crosses over time, making the image feel woven, dynamic and responsive.

## Part 2: Coding Technique Exploration

### Coding Technique: Digital Embroidery in p5.js

<img width="1616" height="1512" alt="cross-stitch-pattern" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/888af6b5-bb05-478c-95d9-988cd29b507c" />

A digital embroidery or cross stitch pattern technique could help create the stitched texture I want to explore. By repeating small “+” and “x” marks, plus signs and short line segments on a grid, the image can feel like it is being stitched rather than simply drawn. This technique fits my project because colour, density, scale and spacing could be adjusted through code. User input could also change the stitch pattern, making the visual texture feel handmade, layered and responsive.
